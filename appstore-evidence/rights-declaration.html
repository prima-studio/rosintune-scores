RosinTune — Statement of Rights for Musical Content

Prepared by [Developer legal name] (ypzhang@stoneridgetechnology.com) · June 10, 2026 · App: RosinTune (iOS)

1. What the app contains

RosinTune is a violin practice tool: it listens through the microphone and gives the player real-time pitch feedback against a target score. Its musical content consists of data files (JSON) describing the notes of public-domain musical works. The sheet music shown on screen is generated at runtime by the app's own music-engraving engine from those data files. The app contains no scans, photographs, or reproductions of any printed edition, no lyrics, and no recordings of third-party performances.

2. Every musical work is in the public domain

Every composition in the app and its content catalog is either a traditional folk melody with no identifiable author or the work of a composer who died in 1888 or earlier (the most recent, Heinrich Ernst Kayser, died over 135 years ago). Every work was first published before 1900. These works are in the public domain in the United States (first published before 1930) and in all life-plus-70-years (and life-plus-100-years) jurisdictions. A complete piece-by-piece manifest follows in the appendix.

3. No third-party edition's content is reproduced

Copyright in sheet music can subsist in a modern edition of a public-domain work (its engraving/typesetting and an editor's added markings) even when the composition itself is free. RosinTune reproduces neither:

  • Engraving and layout are produced entirely by the developer's own software, at runtime, from the note data.
  • The note data is that of the public-domain compositions themselves, prepared with the developer's own conversion pipeline.
  • Editorial markings displayed by the app (fingerings, string numbers, bow-direction marks) are machine-generated by the developer's conversion software — a standard first-position fingering table and alternating bow-stroke assignment. In the étude collections, slurs and articulations are the composers' own composition text (those studies were written to teach exactly those bowing patterns); isolated remaining fingering indications are standard, non-creative position assignments.

4. Titles and metadata

The app and its App Store metadata identify works factually by composer and work title (e.g. "Kayser — 36 Studies, Op. 20"). The app does not use the name of any copyrighted method book, publisher, edition, or arranger, in the app or in its metadata.

5. Declaration

I am the developer of RosinTune and the author of its score data files and rendering software. To the best of my knowledge the app and its content catalog contain no third-party copyrighted material, and I accept responsibility for the content identified above. I am happy to provide any further detail App Review may need.
